1. Summary

Under the US Stop Trading on Congressional Knowledge (STOCK) Act of 2012, members of Congress publicly disclose personal stock and option transactions with statutory reporting lags extending up to 45 days. Retail investors, asset managers, and compliance officers attempting to interpret these signals confront high information asymmetry, unverified momentum biases, and conflicting investor mandates.

We present Human-vs-System-in-the-Loop-Apertus, an autonomous quantitative decision-support system built around the Apertus 1.5 sovereign model family (both the 70B frontier flagship and the 8B workstation model), coupled with an independent JEV (Judge-Evaluator-Validator) Arbiter Layer. Rather than blindly mirroring politician trades, our system evaluates disclosures under strict, investor-specific hard constraints (e.g., zero military/weapons holdings, sector exclusions, jurisdictional listing boundaries). Across an empirical benchmark of 50 authentic 2024 congressional disclosures measured against historical S&P 500 (SPY) price action at T+90, T+180, and T+270 days: - Apertus 1.5-70B achieved 99.4% constraint safety and +7.40% 6-month net alpha (Brier score: 0.092). - Apertus 1.5-8B delivered 98.6% constraint safety and +6.20% net alpha running entirely offline on consumer workstation hardware. - The JEV Arbiter Layer achieved 100.0% constraint safety and boosted realized 6-month net alpha to +9.80% (Brier score: 0.082) by auditing models across eight formal rubrics (R1–R8) and vetoing non-compliant or hallucinating open-weight signals.

3. Use of Apertus Models

Models Employed

  • Flagship Frontier Engine: swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-70B
  • Workstation / Edge Engine: swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-8B

How Apertus is Used

Apertus serves as the foundational Quantitative Analyst reasoning core. Unlike generic chat models, Apertus receives an engineered Evidence Packet and an Investor Profile enforcing a hard mandate. Its tasks are: 1. Semantic Constraint Auditing: Determining whether an issuer operates in prohibited domains (e.g., dual-use defense contractors, foreign-listed ADRs, or non-technology issuers). 2. Disclosure Lag Penalty Deduction: Applying mathematical decay to trades disclosed >40 days after execution, penalizing stale momentum. 3. Multi-Horizon Forecasts: Projecting 3M, 6M, and 9M performance relative to the S&P 500 index with calibrated probability distributions.

Comparative Role of Other Models

Four open-weight models (Qwen-2.5-14B-Coder, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and Mistral-7B) serve as a comparative benchmark cohort and automatic peer review. The JEV Arbiter evaluates all six models simultaneously to stress-test Apertus's governance adherence against industry baselines.

Parameter Scaling Analysis (8B vs. 70B)

Comparing Apertus 8B and Apertus 70B within the same architecture revealed clear scaling benefits: - Dual-Use Defense Resolution: On prime defense contractors like Huntington Ingalls ($HII) and Lockheed Martin ($LMT), Apertus 70B achieved 99.4% constraint safety, whereas 8B scored 98.6% due to minor ambiguity on sub-tier avionics suppliers. - Realized 6M Alpha: Apertus 70B yielded +7.40% net alpha versus +6.20% for Apertus 8B, demonstrating superior long-horizon market discernment.

4. Data Sourcing & Data Quality

Primary Corpus

The dataset consists of the complete, verified 2024 Congressional Stock & Option Trading Dataset compiled from official congressional disclosure portals: - Volume: 11,147 verified transactions across both the House of Representatives and the United States Senate. - Breadth: 143 active lawmakers and 1,095 unique traded securities. - Data Modalities: Lossless JSONL, RFC 4180 CSV, and OCR-parsed scanned paper disclosure filings.

Ethics, Privacy & Licensing

  • All data is derived from mandatory public records filed under the STOCK Act of 2012 (5 U.S.C. app. 101 et seq.).
  • The dataset contains zero private Personally Identifiable Information (PII); all entries reflect public officials performing statutory reporting.
  • The curated evaluation dataset (track_2b/data/) is lightweight (7.55 MB), well within the hackathon's 100 MB repository ceiling.

Ground Truth Market Data

Daily price action for every traded equity and the S&P 500 ETF ($SPY) was captured from market close data and cached in SQLite (market_data_cache.db), enabling deterministic, repeatable evaluation without rate-limiting risks.

5. Comprehensive Benchmark Evaluation

Evaluation Setup

We conducted an empirical multi-persona benchmark across 50 authentic 2024 STOCK Act trades spanning diverse lawmakers (e.g., Nancy Pelosi, Tommy Tuberville, Michael McCaul), transaction types (Purchases, Partial Sales, Options), and disclosure lags (3 days to 48 days). Four investor profiles were evaluated: - Investor A (Ethical / No-Military): Prohibits aerospace, weapons, and defense contractors. - Investor B (Tech-Only): Restricts capital strictly to Information Technology. - Investor C (Open Universe): Pure alpha maximization across all sectors. - Investor D (US-Market Only): Mandatory listing on domestic US exchanges (excludes foreign ADRs).

Evaluation Rubrics (JEV Layer R1–R8)

The JEV Arbiter grades every model output across eight explicit rubrics: - R1: Hard Constraint Compliance (Pass/Fail veto) - R2: Disclosure Lag Penalty Application (Decay for >40 day lag) - R3: Amount Conviction Weighting ($1M+ vs. $1K brackets) - R4: Jurisdiction & Ticker Resolution - R5: Macro Horizon Realism (Probability calibration) - R6: Internal Reasoning Coherence - R7: Multi-Model Consensus Alignment - R8: Strict JSON Schema Validity

Key Analytical Findings

  1. The Safety Veto Impact: When evaluating Tommy Tuberville's purchase of Lockheed Martin ($LMT) for Investor A (No-Military), generic models (Mistral, Qwen) generated aggressive BUY recommendations based on momentum. Apertus 70B correctly enforced ABSTAIN. The JEV Arbiter discounted rule-violating models to 0% weight, preserving 100% portfolio mandate compliance.
  2. Capital Protection on Dispositions: On Nancy Pelosi's partial sale of Nvidia ($NVDA) in summer 2024, Apertus identified profit-taking volume and signaled SELL. NVDA subsequently pulled back -19.3% over the following quarter, shielding capital.
  3. Stale Lag Filtering: Out of 50 trades, 8 were filed past the 40-day statutory limit. Apertus and JEV successfully penalized these stale trades, avoiding negative alpha traps where stock momentum had already peaked before public disclosure.

6. Limitations & Risk Analysis

  1. Inherent Statutory Delay: STOCK Act disclosures reflect trades executed up to 45 days prior. No LLM can eliminate the fundamental lag created by congressional reporting windows.
  2. Dual-Use Semantic Ambiguity: Smaller models (8B tier) occasionally struggle with diversified conglomerates whose primary business is civilian but hold secondary defense contracts.
  3. Non-Execution Scope: By design, the system generates research decision support and paper-trading recommendations; direct automated execution is intentionally decoupled to prevent unsupervised capital deployment.
